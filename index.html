Reading these results

Overflow is water the tier could not take fast enough (intake limit = structures × rock permeability × contact area × gradient).

Held in dry rock applies only to tiers above the water table: part of the water stays behind and the rest arrives late.

Boundary loss is water that drifted out of the model area. A tier that loses a lot this way will not give the water back at the well.

Every number is a comparison with the same run without cloudburst water, so the effect of the water is separated from the effect of the other assumptions. Not calibrated; not a yield.

What do you want to see

Recharge well design

Used by the net zero plan, one storm and season views. The contact area of each well is worked out from its shape and the depth band it is open to: shallow wells are shafts or pits in the 0–20 m saprolite; moderate and deep wells are borewells, cased above their tier and open over the whole tier.

1 · Land and rain on the lease

2 · Reservoir

The reservoir holds what the land runoff and roofs deliver. There is no overflow basin: anything the reservoir cannot hold would leave the lease, so the reservoir is sized for the design storm. Change the size and press Calculate.

3 · Roof tanks: three chambers per flat building, and the amenity tank design proposal

The roof of the flat buildings fills chamber A first, then chamber B; what neither can hold overflows to the reservoir. Water is drawn from chamber B first, then the amenity tank, the reservoir and chamber A last, so A stays as the pure-water reserve and B is the chamber that is empty when a storm comes. In the storm season (default September to November) B is used first and not refilled with pure water, so the share kept empty is free space for a storm; the Net zero storm check takes that space as relief, but only for storms in the season. Outside the season B takes rain and, with top-up on, pure water made from treated wastewater that has no other use. The flush chamber (toilet-flushing water, about ten days of use) holds the reverse-osmosis concentrate first and treated wastewater after it; it does not change the water balance. Overhead tanks and the amenity building's own demand are not modelled.

4 · What empties the reservoir (recharge wells)

Total intake of each well set in m³/d, worked out with the recharge well design above. Base wells: register rock K. Recharge-zone sets: the rock K at each ADMT recharge-zone point and tier, read back from the saved cloudburst runs. Candidate zones, not confirmed until drilling and pumping tests. A value you type is kept (yellow) and no longer follows the design.

Flushing, irrigation and the fountain are supplied first from treated wastewater, then from the reservoir. Drinking, bathing, cooking and washing are supplied from roof tanks and the reservoir; what is left is the outside supply.

What this tab does, why, and how to use it

What

It compares the candidate sites (A, B, C ...) as places to put the cloudburst recharge structures. For every site it brings together three separate layers: storability of the rock in each depth tier (specific yield, the share of rock volume that can fill with water), connectivity of the passive-ADMT candidate zones (M6), and the cloudburst water simulation (MODFLOW 6): how much of one storm day of roof water reaches the water table, is held in dry rock, and is still stored after 1 and 10 years.

Why

High storability alone does not make a good recharge spot: the water must reach it, stay in it, and not drain out of the model. The layers are shown side by side and never merged into one score. For every comparison the tab also shows in what share of the parameter sets each site keeps the most water, so a small difference is not read as a real one.

How

1) Tick the sites and press Start the cloudburst simulations at these sites (a quick screening takes minutes; a calculation with uncertainty takes longer). 2) Choose the ADMT reading, the model boundary, the tier and what to compare. 3) Read the chart, the table and the map, and the plain-language reading. Recharge points are candidate zones for connected fractured rock inferred from passive geophysical prospecting (ADMT), not confirmed until drilling and pumping tests.

Recharge zone (RZ) points, found from storability

Drilling and recharge are two different questions. The sites A, B, C ... are candidate borewell locations (priority and reserve). The RZ points are candidate recharge locations: for each of the four tiers and for each ADMT reading that has a permeable class (positive and neutral; the negative reading has none), the storability of the rock is mapped over the surveyed ground inside the lease, away from the boundary and the transformer noise zone, and the best points are taken. Each point is tagged with the nearest interpreted ADMT zone and whether that zone reaches the tier depth. The readings are never combined: the column Found in lists every tier and reading that picked the point.
